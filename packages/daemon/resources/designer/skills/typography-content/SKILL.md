name: typography-content
description: "Use when setting text hierarchy, headings, labels, numbers and microcopy for product screens, or when a mock reads as cluttered or generic."
---

Hierarchy comes from size, weight, space and position, in that order; color comes last. Use the contract's type scale (`--design-size-*`) and do not invent sizes.

- One h1 per page that names the place or the object. Section headings describe their content ("Failed checks"), not their container ("Panel 2").
- Keep body text to 60–80 characters per line. Secondary text uses `--design-muted` and is never smaller than the caption size.
- Labels are nouns. Buttons are verbs with objects ("Save schedule", not "Submit"). Use sentence case; no all-caps paragraphs.
- Numbers: tabular figures in columns, consistent units and precision, thousands separators, right alignment in tables. Show "—" when a value is not available and say why nearby.
- Status is a word plus color or an icon ("Failed", "Needs review").
- Empty and error copy says what happened, why, and what to do next, in one or two short sentences. No blame and no filler such as "Oops!" or "Something went wrong".
- Dates: relative for recent events ("5 min ago") with the exact time available; absolute for schedules and deadlines.
- Truncate only when the full text is reachable another way. A title attribute is not enough on touch screens; wrap or link to a detail view.

Remove words before adding style. Generic marketing phrases, lorem ipsum and placeholder names are review findings.
